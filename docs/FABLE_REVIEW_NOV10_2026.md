# Beta / Swiggy Instamart: one independent engineering review

Prepared 10 November 2026, India time. Scope: 31 October–10 November. Recipient: Fable, read-only. Please answer from this packet in one response, without opening files, requesting customer data, calling tools, or editing anything. This is an engineering checkpoint, not a release recommendation. Examples below are test cases, not a shopper's purchase history.

## The decision we need

Beta is for an older adult who may speak or type a grocery list in Indian English or a mix of languages. The product promise is **one understandable review of the entire basket**: what was heard, what will be bought, how many retail units, pack size, price, what could not be matched, and delivery destination before committing. A request to buy two 100 g packets is not a request to buy the nearest 200 g product. No automatic substitution, rounding up, dietary reinterpretation, payment, or retry of a write with unknown outcome. Not every request is fulfillable. Distinguish a faithful proposal the shopper can review, an interpretation that needs clarification, a stock miss and an uncertain system result.

Our latest phone preview is Android 0.3.4 (version code 21). On 10 November the exact private APK was installed in place, retaining the app's data; the phone returned Swiggy READY, HTTP 200. The preview service reported both cart writes and checkout disabled. **Today's check was connection only.** No new item, cart, address or payment test was run for this checkpoint. We are not at 90–95% qualified whole-basket usability.

## What changed during these ten days

1. **Preserved source evidence instead of trusting the model's rewrite.** The shared interpretation contract associates item, quantity, unit, per-pack size, original wording and scope. Tests exercise original native-script units, dimensions, numbers used as descriptions rather than counts, and repeated/ambiguous quantities. Model agreement alone is not proof. Quantity provenance reused across native processing removed six replay exceptions. In mixed-script DEV, a formerly blocked two-line review progressed from zero to both exact lines without dropping the quantity guard.
2. **Kept named product attributes distinct from dietary promises.** Checks cover unsalted vs salt-free/no salt, no-added vs free of, and sugar-free vs unsweetened, in both request directions where applicable. Conflicting descriptions for the same SKU veto selection. A registered literal identity/term can help retrieve a candidate; it cannot certify its composition or override an exclusion.
3. **Improved how store facts reach Android.** Direct child/variation names, size descriptions and current selling prices must belong to the same purchasable variant, not leak in from a sibling or parent. One synthetic realistic four-basket group originally withheld all four; later correctly priced two-line replays improved 0/4 to 3/4 on a particular identical-reply exercise, retaining all seven negative controls. Exact division of a requested total by a proven sale-unit size is allowed; rounding up is not.
4. **Separated parent identity from variant evidence and food form from counted units.** An opaque retailer parent identifier can group variants without standing in for the chosen SKU. “Cheese slices” is a product form, not necessarily an extra count. Acceptance needs the same SKU all the way through final review. The latest parent-ID fix only improved nominations; it has not had current-source hosted or whole-basket verification.
5. **Improved the measuring instrument.** Label first-pass blind vs previously seen, seller assessment vs nomination, app vs mocked catalogue, and exact reviewed quantities/prices vs suggestions. Retain timeout/hard errors as such. No fabricated prevalence, latency or cost estimate. Benchmark inputs are not being published as an answer key in this brief.

## Our proposed path to completion — challenge it

1. Start with a small **typed contract**: requested product identity or named edition, literal evidence span, explicit must-have / avoid claim, preparation/form, brand preference vs requirement, outer retail count vs inner net amount, and total physical amount. Keep “unknown” separate from false. Ask the language model for one structured interpretation; keep proof checks deterministic, do not let it place orders. Validate against the original utterance once rather than repeatedly guessing what an already validated number meant.
2. Normalize a **current seller offer** into the same dimensions and preserve attribution: SKU, brand field, variant title, form, single sale unit vs multipack, per-unit content, current price/currency, availability and timestamp. Use a small versioned reviewed vocabulary or retrieval index of observed public product terms if it improves recall; use fresh address-scoped catalogue data for facts. Do not crawl all of Swiggy, build a customer-order corpus or treat similarity as dietary proof. Make purchase history a tie-breaker after hard requirements. Provide one review showing material uncertainty and unused words; explicit change/omission requires consent.
3. Freeze interface, configuration, source and binary; replay failures **as development**. Separate parsing holds, discovery misses, stock unavailable, quantity/claim contradictions, seller uncertainty and external faults. Use an independent held-out panel with ordinary single/short lists, 15–20-item lists, Hindi/Hinglish and southern-language code-switches, phonetic spellings, explicit brand vs generic, repeated numbers, dietary and substitution negatives. Score one first attempt, all lines strictly correct, exact retail counts and grounded prices; no dropping rows from the denominator. Record both stocked-only and all-request outcomes, unsafe acceptance and unnecessary holds, plus end-to-end median/tail latency and cost per completed review. Pre-register sample size/decision interval; don't keep retuning a “blind” set.
4. Only after the above improves: verify the **same source** packaged in an isolated hosted preview and the same APK on the phone (current-session, interrupted-session, voice-to-text continuation, no location/distant address, stock changes, 15–20 lines). Keep write and checkout switches off in this phase. Require a separately authorized, reversible cart-only validation of exact address, preexisting cart, intended diff and readback before any rollout. Never infer payment or duplicate-prevention safety from a preview. Roll out with a kill switch and clear withdrawal threshold, not with “95%” inferred from unit tests.

## Questions for Fable — please prioritize instead of proposing more rules

1. Identify the **two or three smallest general changes** that unlock the most verified whole baskets. Which current layers or repeated Kotlin/Python checks can be removed safely? What belongs once in the backend contract, what must the phone independently verify immediately before cart action?
2. When someone requests one ordinary “pack” and Swiggy sells one purchasable SKU with a reliable 500 g net size, what distinguishes a defensible **one sale-unit proposal**, a required clarification and a hard veto? Give a compact decision table contrasting generic pack, specifically named carton, explicit multiple inner packs, “two packs of 100 g each”, total 200 g, ambiguous seller fields, dietary and allergy claims. Can a low-risk review show all proposals without silently changing an order or requiring many questions?
3. Should literal catalogued product identity precede free-text attribute interpretation (e.g. unsalted butter)? How to represent separate maker, edition and ingredient claims without a hard-coded SKU dictionary? Is bounded store-scoped discovery/cache or retrieval worthwhile, given coverage, bias, stale stock, upstream terms and price freshness? What would falsify that investment?
4. How should we combine language-model interpretation, independent evidence, purchase-history preference and deterministic acceptance without multiplicative false negatives on longer lists? Which false negatives are justifiable? How should uncertainty be communicated to an older adult who wants one review, including unavailable items, without making a misleading claim of completion?
5. What adversarial-but-defensive test design, blind sampling and release gate would support ≥90% **new** stocked whole baskets with an aspiration of 95% basic? What sample, confidence interval, latency budget, provider timeout/fallback policy, absolute safety stops and rollback criteria would you choose? Explain how not to count three provider calls on the same order as three independent users.
6. Security / privacy design review: treat an APK-embedded service header as public, not a user identity. How to bind account and installation, scope tokens to the authorized shopper, rotate/refresh without sign-in loops, prevent cross-user history/cache leakage and cap spend per account/install? What minimum controls are needed for OAuth state/PKCE, replay resistance, seller/model content as untrusted data, and safe device logs/analytics without addresses, raw utterances or cart contents?
7. When writes return later, what is the minimal idempotency/readback and address/price reconfirmation contract when the native retailer app or another session changes a cart? What must happen on an unknown write result, a stale address or price, multiple taps, app restart and uncertain payment? How to make production vs preview isolation, operational budgets and kill switches testable?
